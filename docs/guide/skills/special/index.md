# Special Skills

Dusklight characters gain special skills when they select backgrounds, by focusing on specific talents while on their adventure, or by encountering unique circumstances in the world. While basic skills set all adventurers apart from civilians, special skills are where the true heroes of the realm start to shine.

Because these skills provide additional stat upgrades and capabilities, they often come with additional conditions. Some, often the most powerful, can even come along with an ongoing negative effect. It's important to watch for these sorts of details when choosing your skills, though you won't always have the opportunity to choose.

Special skills come in three main types, differentiated by their source: Background based, Training based, and Opportunity based. We'll go into each category in a bit more detail below.

## Background Based Skills

Aside from basic skills, these are what you start your campaign with. They are the foundation of who your character will become, and also reflect where they've been before the campaign began. Some background skills may only be obtained through background selection, so pay attention to what you're picking up.

Let's say for example you pick a **Circus Performer** background, your character will automatically get skills like **Crowd Pleaser** which increases your charm, or **Daring Feats** which gives you a boost to dexterity when you're doing something that seems impossible.

That's a somewhat contrived example, but you start to get the idea of how your character might come together. What you can do at the start of the campaign is driven by who you were before it.

## Training Based Skills

Some background skills, and a wide variety of other specialized skills, can be obtained during the campaign through training. In effect, this means that *all* characters have a level 0 in *all* trainable skills, since they can train into them but haven't invested any into leveling those skills up.

Training this type of skill can be done in one of two ways. Your first option is to just start using the skill in the campaign. Since you haven't leveled the skill up it won't grant you any effect, but if you can provide a narrative reasoning for how you're using the skill, you can count it as a use. While some skills will prohibit you from doing so, most can also be trained as a [Downtime](../../systems/downtime.md) activity, allowing you to gain abilities without as much risk.

Each time you use a skill it has a chance at increasing in level, This is determined by the Keeper. When a skill reaches level 1, you've officially trained into it and can start using it effectively in your campaign.

## Opportunity Based Skills

The rarest kind of skill is the opportunity based one. These only show up when you've completed a specific set of requirements in your campaign as a character, which vary based on the skill. Opportunity based skills do not start at level 0, and characters only have access to them after being granted the skill directly.

Opportunity skills will show up as things like boons from your deity, or specific benefits granted by unique items in the world. They often come with substantial base stat adjustments, and at higher levels can have world changing active abilities.

Because these skills often come from arrangements made with powers greater than you, it's important to keep track of the fine print. This type of skill is the most likely to have negative consequences attached to it.
