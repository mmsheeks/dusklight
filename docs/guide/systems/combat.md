# Turn Based Combat

Dusklight uses a turn-based combat system with D&D like turn order. This should be pretty familiar for most players. Instead of a main and bonus action however, players have action points they can spend on their turn, giving a bit more flexibility to the flow of combat.

## Turn Order

Turn order is decided based on a dice roll. Everyone in the turn order rolls a **dexterity** check, placing them in order for combat from highest to lowest. If two characters tie, the character with the higher base **dexterity** score goes first. If their base scores are also tied, resolve the order using conditionally modified scores or just let the players decide.

## On Your Turn

On each character or NPC's turn they have three total action points they can spend. These can be spent to use a skill, take a generic action, or for movement. You may also hold one action point to spend after your turn.

### Generic Actions

Not everything is so fancy as to need a skill. Sometimes you're just punching a guy. Or move a box. Or closing a door. That's a generic action.

### Using a Skill

Most skills cost one action point to use. Some may cost more, and others are entirely free. Depending on the skill, other conditions may need to be met as well.

When you use action points to use a skill, roll the relevant check for the active skill ability and resolve the action based on the text of the skill.

### Movement

Movement during combat is limited, and characters can generally only travel 30 feet per action point spent on movement each turn. Some skills will modify this value, and you may spend all three action points on movement if you wish.

## Off Turn Actions

Each turn you may hold up to one action point to be spent off of your turn. This can either be spent in reaction to a specific trigger, or to gain the **ready** status.

### Triggered Reactions

Triggered reactions can be anything normally accomplished on your turn with a single action point. You must set the trigger for the reaction during your turn, and the action point expires at the start of your next turn if you don't use it.

### Ready Status

Instead of setting up a triggered action, you may also hold one action point to be considered **ready**. This means if an enemy moves within range or makes an attack against you, you can spend this action point to react before their action resolves. You cannot use the ready state to move. If you do not spend the action point before your next turn, it expires.

## Surprise Conditions

If a party manages to get the jump on an enemy, each member gets a single turn with a reduced pool of 2 action points they can spend before combat begins. These can be taken in any order, but no action points can be held.
