# Building a Character

Characters are made up of essentially their background, stat points, and acquired skills. All characters have access to basic skills, while more advanced skills have requirements to train and use.

## Core Stats

To get started, let's go over a character's core statistics. These five stats all have a starting base value. When you create your character, you will roll **2d6** to generate a pool of stat points you can distribute among them.

### Strength 

Base Score: 1

Strength does what it says. The more you have, the stronger you are. Your average civilian has a strength of 1. A well trained body builder likely has a strength between 6 and 10.

### Smarts

Base Score: 1

Are you clever? Or maybe you just know a lot of things. Either way, Smarts is what you've invested in. Characters with a high Smarts stat are sometimes seen as a bit stuffy, but when things get complicated they're who you need.

### Magic

Base Score: 0

How attuned are you to the wild ways of the world? Have you made a deal with a spirit? Offered your services to a god? This is where that power resides, from minor healing magic to major summonings and more.

### Charm

Base Score: 1

Can you dazzle the crowd? Charm the shopkeep? Talk your way out of the worst situations? A silver tongue and a quick wit are reflected in your Charm skill.

### Dexterity

Base Score: 1

Being quick on your feet and flexible in tight spaces has its perks. Make yourself harder to hit, dance your way around obstacles, or find your way around delicate traps and mechanisms. Whatever the situation calls for.

## Special Stats

These stats aren't rolled like the rest, and are instead each handled in their own specific way.

### Character Health

Your health is a function of your Strength, and an non-rolled base stat called **Endurance**. All characters have a base Endurance of 10. You add 2 times your strength score to your base endurance to get your maximum health.

As you might notice, that's a pretty small starting HP pool, averaging around 12-14. You shouldn't expect your character to feel much more durable than this at any given point in your playthrough. Dusklight is a game with real risks. You will get stronger, but so will the world around you. It is important to approach combat strategically, lest you need to learn more about [Character death](../systems/character-death.md).

### Luck

All characters also have a expendable resource pool called Luck. Characters start out with a full pool of 10 Luck, which they can spend to various effects during sessions. It is rare to increase the amount of luck available, and the resource does not regularly replenish itself, so spend wisely.

Characters who have fully expended their luck may have various negative effects applied, based on their acquired skills and other aspects of their build. These will show up as [Curse](../skills/special/curses.md) effects.
