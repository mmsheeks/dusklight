# The History of Alue

!!! danger "SPOILERS"
    **HEADS UP!** Everything on this page is a spoiler for the current setting of the Dusklight world. Read on at your own risk.

## Earliest Days

Before the splendor captured in stone today existed, there was a simple farming town. Alue, named for the river that ran along side it, which had been named long before anyone who lived there remembers, was that town. Set in the northern reaches of a vast and fertile landscape, the town was far from any neighboring kingdoms or villages.

The people of the town, hard working and happy in a simple and safe home, spent their days farming the fields, turning their crops into food, and crafting the necessities for living a comfortable life in an easy land.

The town grew, of course, as all settlements of humans do. A few families became a few dozen. Some families left - traveled south, east, west - and settled in new places, starting new farms, and building new villages. Others stayed, building homes at the edge of town, making them the new edge of the town, and spreading ever outward.

Eventually, the families of Alue and their decendents in the neighboring villages set up roads for easier travel. The town, now a city proper, built trading halls for exchanging resources with its sister cities and great halls for hosting celebrations and gatherings. 

This can, in some ways, be marked as the start of the Aluen Empire, though none who lived within it would yet see themselves in such a role. For the citizens of Alue, they simply worked together towards a common goal of living well upon the plains. It would not be for several generations that a conflict would arise to force their hand to more formal agreements.

## War of Seven Sisters

After three hundred years of expansion and unorganized coordination, the time of such peaceful arangements came to pass. Who is to blame for the beginning of the civil war, but the aims of all involved were clear: Come out on top, and claim ownership of the now fully developed kingdom that was once the city of Alue.

The War of Seven Sisters, named for the seven major cities of the Aluen Kingdom that lead the combat, was a brutal and drawn out conflict. While traditional fronts were built between the cities with trenches and lines of soldiers with the impliments of war fought for control on various fronts, a more insidious form of combat took center stage in the later years of the war.

Assassination attempts ran rampant in the late stages of the conflict. Leaders of the various cities largely went into hiding, though that did not save them all. When leaders couldn't be found, some of the more brutally minded armies would turn their assassins on whatever civilians could be found. The combination of the loss of leadership and morale over being abandonned to such brutal treatement spelled the end of the ruling families, one way or another.

When the war ended, only three cities remained. Alue, whose fortification walls were never breached; Botto, whose armies only ever fought in defense of their own lands; and Cinde, who in the end yielded to Alue after having conguered many of their neighboring towns.

## Building an Empire

According to the written history of Alue, the end of the War of Seven Sisters was the true beginning of the Aluen Empire. In an effort to build a structure that would not lead to such a conflict again, formal accords were struck between the remaining cities as to how the land would be shared and governed. These became known as the Aluen Accords, and set the foundation fo the empire.

Under the accords, each of the families in charge of a surviving city would remain in control of their current holdings for the next fifty years. During this time, each would commit resources in equal part to rebuilding what was lost during the conflict. Once that time had passed, the ruling families were replaced with an elected parliment. While Alue remained the functional seat of the government, all Seven Sisters - now rebuilt - held equal voting rights in the decisions and laws governing the new Aluen Empire.

It was under this arrangement that the Aluen Empire grew most voraciously. Expanding eastward until they came in contact with the Elven free cities in the [Lowlands Forest](../regions/eastern-woodlands/lowlands-forest.md), and reaching West all the way to the coastline. The vast expanse of the Aluen Empire provided safe home to many generations, and saw the development of many new technologies as well as the dawn of mortal knowledge of magic.

## Magical Age of Discovery

With the expansion of the mortal empire to the edges of the trading lands of the elves, two distinct histories and understandings of the Magic of the world came into contact with one another. The elves, long holding traditions of magic that stem from both spiritual and deific sources, practiced a form of magic that derives its power from other planes. Mortals, having dabbled in the earliest forms of alchemy and simple runic healing magic, believed magic an inate element of the world to be tapped into and used.

Many resources were put into researching how to combine the clearly more vast and intense magic of the elves with the runic understandings of mortals. While some objected to this study for safety or religious reasons, the effort was widely shared by both mortals and elves as a work in good faith to expand mutual understanding of their shared world.

This research lead to an ocean of discoveries and developments in new magic. Elven spells that once took weeks of ritual preperation could be stored in five runestones and cast in minutes if arranged correctly. Mortal healing magic that was once limited to the abstract energy in the air around them could now tap into deep and plentiful wells of magical power from other planes.

Early concerns about upsetting the balance of power between the planes or angering the gods by using their magics without proper ritual proved unfounded, and so the research continued. Newly lifted sanctions on experimenting with different forms of magical casting lead to new ways of using runes and ritual alike. Eventually, mortal magic became a powerful enough tool that one could commune with the gods themselves, given you had enough resources to cast the runes.

### Blood Magic

It was during this age that a new way to satiate the demands of the runes was found, and blood magic was born. Traditionally, one must carve the runes of power into stones and inlay the carvings with a sufficiently rare mineral to form a connection to the plane of the god whose power you seek. If, however, you carve the same sigil into the skin of another living creature, the same spell can be cast. The cost, however, is extracted in equal measure from the life of the creature the rune is carved into.

This can be useful for emergency simple spells, and was seen as largely harmless by early public expirements. These took essentially the form of a tattoo carved into a willing participant for a simple thing like a light spell. When activated, the scar of the carving would burn for a moment, but the spell would cast. So long as the demand of the spell was not too great, the natural healing of the body could easily outpace its use.

The practice was outlawed shortly after the first experiment with a higher order spell. By chaining multiple runes together in a complex sigil, spells of higher order can be cast. However, the more complex a spell, the higher the cost of an activation. An early expirement into this lead to the death of the subject, and the practice of using blood sigils was banned shortly after.

## Early Arcane Wars

While general agreements between the Aluen Empire and their neighboring lands had been struck and were largely holding, there were areas of conflict along the border that sprung up from time to time. These conflicts were usually over territorial disputes, though sometimes they were driven by disagreements in magical practice as well.

While the parliament back in Alue did their best to squash these conflicts as they sprung up, they struggled to keep control over a border so distant from their physical rule. Over time, these conflicts built into a slow and quiet but deadly war. This time, unlike before, the mortals of the Aluen Empire were not restrained by only having access to simple healing magic, and the conflict got messy very quickly.

## Alue's Great Peace

As the magical attacks between the bordering lands grew more intense and gruesome, the leaders of both the Empire and their neighboring kingdoms came together to enforce a desperate but iron clad solution, proposed by none other than the senior minister from Alue herself. This was the binding spell known as Alue's Great Peace.

Carved into the very foundations of the palace in Alue, the great craftsmen and mages of the Aluen Empire would cast a runic sigil that would hold a spell unlike any other. The sigil, combined with an activation ritual performed by the Elven high priests to call upon their deity of binding to empower the stonework, would enforce a magical contract upon all of reality.

Alue's Great Peace would ensure that no living being could use magic to intentionally cause harm to another. Not by agreement, not by mutual understanding, but by true magical binding. A spell cast once, in one place, meant to bind all living beings to one rule forever.

The effort was put to a vote, which it passed. The work was completed, the ritual cast, and the contract took hold. For two hundred years, Alue's Great Peace kept the conflict at bay.

## The Breaking of the Peace

But like all things, Alue's Great Peace also had an end. Over time, arcane research continued to develop. More powerful magics were discovered, and while none could intentionally levey it against another, efforts around that were regularly made. It was only a matter of time until someone found a way to break through the spell proper.

This was done, ultimately, by a lone mage in a quiet room working by magelight in the dark of the night. One of dozens who had been working, for one lord or another, to find a way to break the peace and take the world for themselves. For after all, if everyone else is bound to peace, but you found the way out, who can stop you?

How exactly the peace was broken is lost to time, but the result was catastrophic and instant. The moment the first mortal magic was cast with intent to harm, the sigil at the palace of Alue was broken and the ritual agreement made took its toll. Alue, and everything living within it, was turned instantly into a bright white stone that would never age, break or deteriorate. A forever reminder of the cost of breaking promises made to the gods.

## The Fall of Aluen

While Alue itself was gone the moment the peace was broken, the Empire that had built around it took centuries to deteriorate. These dark years were filled with fits and starts of attempts to rebuild society in the wake of the breaking, but none ever found foothold.

An ever rising arms race of magical weaponry left the land torn and strewn with bodies and blood. Eventually, in a spiteful rage in the final moments of a losing battle against the elves, the last mages of Botto cast a spell that sundered the heavens and let the rage of all the gods through to our world. This was the cause of the first Nocturne.
