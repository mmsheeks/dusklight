# Old Alue

At the northern edge of the [Golden Plains](index.md) sits a city cast in stone. Though the roads that lead to it have become varying degrees of overgrown, save the ones built and maintained by todays society, the city itself gleams in near perfect condition. A perfect preservation in white stone of an entire city that stops abruptly just at the edge of the still imposing fortification wall. Old Alue stands as a remnant of a society that the world has long forgotten, its name only surviving through the precisely preserved stone of the signs and banners of the old city.

## A Moment in Stone

While no living residents of this land can trace their lineage to Alue, the stone city is clearly the site of some long forgotten catastrophe. To get the idea across, you first need a sense of scale.

Alue is a sprawling city, rivaling the capitals of many modern nations in size and marvels. At its center is a grand palace with a dozen flying spires rising above grand halls and chambers to scrape against the sky of the Golden Plains. As one moves out from the palace the city flows in almost spiraling rings towards the outer walls. Market squares, rows of wealthy residences, a vast craft works quarter with housing and market stalls, shipping warehouses, stables for regular visitors to the city, an entertainment district. It is believed that Alue was once the seat of some grand nation, long since forgotten.

This history serves to illustrate a point. The entire city, now rendered in stone, is full of statues of people, pets, and livestock. People going about their day, frozen in place as though they were mid conversation, mid step, mid bite in their afternoon meal. The once great city of Alue turned to stone in a single moment, and no one within seemed to see it coming.

## Preservation Stone

The white city that Old Alue is made of was once rumored to be a rare and imported material, brought to our land by a great artist aiming to create a sculpture that would confuse the world for generations to come. This story, while still told by some artisans in the countryside, has been largely disproven. More recent alchemical and magical research has found a way to replicate the material through a combination of specific stasis spells and simultaneous material conversion using gold and other rare alloys. 

While modern Preservation Stone has proven reliable, being used in many large structures throughout the land that have stood the test of decades of wear, it still eventually shows its age and requires upkeep to maintain. The stone of Old Alue recieves no such care that any living record can prove, yet it does not show any signs of age. Samples that have been removed for aging routinely read as though the stone was just created, mere moments ago, regardless of when they are tested. Stranger still, the areas that have been sampled return to their original shape. These properties remain unexplained and no modern magic or science has been able to replicate the effect. This more powerful form of the material was given the name True Preservation Stone, or True Stone, and continues to be one of the many materials that practitioners of the arcane arts seek to replicate.

### Stone Sickness

While early attempts were made to abuse the seemingly limitless regrowth of the True Stone of Old Alue, the practice of taking the stone from the site has since been entirely forbidden. The material does re-grow where it once was, and the sample that was taken remains, making the supply genuinely bottomless. Keeping such samples around populated areas, however, has proven to have a negative effect on all living creatures nearby.

This effect was given the name Stone Sickness, and varries in presentation based on the creature it is impacting. For most sentient life, Stone Sickness presents itself as an incurable weariness, increased agitation, and a reduction in general social cohesion. The degree of the effect seems to vary by individual, but proximity to the taken stone, duration of exposure, and the sheer quantity of the material present, all seem to also play into the intensity of the effect.

??? warning "SPOILERS"

    Stone Sickness was first named by researchers responding to the Camtuk Incident. The town of Camtuk, south-east of Old Alue, had become a hub for resource extraction from the ancient site following the discovery of True Stone's superior properties.

    The Kingsguard, responding to reports of general unrest and disrepair in the town, arrived to find most of the towns residents laying throughout the village, malnourished, in various degrees of exhaustion, and ready to fight anyone offering them aid.

    The Camtuk Incident, as it became known, is also the namesake of the required handling protocol for those now handling True Stone under approved extraction and research projects.
